CodeTrail in the classroom

A free game where pupils program a little hero to the goal with cards. No login, no installation, no data collection. The language follows the device and can be set under Settings.

Open the game: codetrail.dk
Age groupGrades 0-4 (the first two difficulty tiers from about age 5)
SubjectsComputing / digital literacy, mathematics, enrichment lessons
Time20-45 minutes per session, repeatable (levels are always new)
DevicesChromebook, PC, Mac or a recent iPad in the browser. Works with mouse and touch.
PriceFree, no ads

What do pupils learn?

  • Algorithmic thinking: breaking a task into small steps in the right order.
  • Directions and perspective: first up/down/left/right, later "turn left" as seen by the hero.
  • Debugging: the program runs, the hero goes wrong, the pupil finds and fixes the mistake.
  • Loops and blocks: "repeat 3 times" and a reusable "block A" on the last two tiers.
  • Optimisation: more stars for fewer cards, so pupils look for the shortest solution.

Difficulty tiers

1 First stepsArrows up, down, right, left. Short paths.
2 Longer pathsSame arrows, longer paths and more cards to keep track of.
3 Turn and go"Forward X" and turn left / right as seen by the hero.
4 Jump overHop over water and rocks.
5 Loops"Repeat ×N" with cards inside. Bigger boards.
6 BlocksBuild "block A" once and call it several times.

Two modes: Build the path (the pupil writes the program) and Guess the finish (the pupil reads a finished program and points at where the hero ends). The second one trains reading code.

Running a lesson

  1. Show the game on the board for 3 minutes: create a profile, pick "First steps", solve one level together.
  2. Pupils open codetrail.dk, type their name as a profile and pick the same tier.
  3. 15-20 minutes on their own. The ? badge on the card tray explains the cards, the bulb gives a hint.
  4. Finish by asking: "Who solved a level with the fewest cards? How?"
  5. Next time: one tier up. "Guess the finish" works well as a whole-class task on the board.

Practical notes

  • Progress is saved in the browser on each device. If pupils share devices, a profile can be exported as a code under Profiles and imported elsewhere.
  • No accounts, no e-mail, nothing leaves the device. More: privacy policy.
  • Also available as an app for Windows, macOS, Linux and Android: github.com/acidsnare/CodeTrail/releases.
  • Languages under Settings: Danish, English, Romanian, Russian, Ukrainian.
